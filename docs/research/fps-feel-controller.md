# FPS feel prior art: console shooters on a controller

This is a reference for making reVC's first-person mode aim well on an Xbox or PlayStation pad. It covers right-stick look handling, aim assist, and shooter "game feel" techniques. Numbers are given wherever a source states them.

The mod today uses a small round dead zone and a power curve (exponent 1.7 hip, 2.0 aiming). Its full-deflection turn rate is 3.5 rad/s (≈200°/s) yaw and 2.2 rad/s (≈126°/s) pitch, slower while aiming.

## Sources and how far to trust them

**Primary** means the developer's own code, documentation, patch notes or talk. **Secondary** means press, a wiki, a dataminer or a community guide. Secondary claims are marked inline, each with a note on how far to trust it. A value marked **derived** was computed by me from a source's formula and is not quoted. A value marked **judgment** is my recommendation.

## 1. Stick input handling

### 1.1 Dead zones: shape, inner and outer

- **Axial vs radial vs scaled radial.** An axial dead zone zeroes each axis separately, which "snaps" sweeping aim to the cardinal directions. A radial one zeroes the whole vector below the threshold. That fixes the snapping, but the output jumps from 0 straight to the threshold value. A scaled radial dead zone rescales what is left, `out = dir * (len − dz) / (1 − dz)`, so output ramps up from zero. Sutphin calls that version "the right way". Typical thresholds are "somewhere between 0.1 to 0.2". An OUYA pad needed 0.25, while a new Xbox 360 pad was fine at about 0.1. Source: [Josh Sutphin, "Doing Thumbstick Dead Zones Right", Game Developer, 2013-04-16](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/doing-thumbstick-dead-zones-right). Sutphin was lead designer on Starhawk.
- **"Bowtie" for FPS aim.** This blends a scaled radial zone with an axial one in which "the stronger your input in one axis, the larger the dead zone gets for the other axis". A horizontal sweep then stays on its line. LightBox used it; the article gives no code. Source: same article.
- **Which shape to use.** Use independent axes when the axes drive unrelated things, such as steering and throttle. Use circular when the stick picks a point on a plane, "such as aiming a weapon". Source: [Shawn Hargreaves, "Gamepads suck", 2007-03-28](https://www.shawnhargreaves.com/blog/gamepads-suck.html) (XNA team).

### 1.2 Response curves

- **Doom 3 BFG** ships `joy_gammaLook 1`. Despite its "log" label, that mode squares the input after the dead zone (power **2**). The other options are linear and an "exponential" curve, 1.0471^(100x) × 0.01. That equals 100^(x−1) (**derived**), which gives 0.1 at half stick. Both sticks use the same function. Source: [D3BFG `UsercmdGen.cpp` L37–38, L745–756](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/framework/UsercmdGen.cpp#L745-L756).
- **Microsoft** recommends a non-linear transform after the dead zone. Its example is cubing the input for driving, which gives "more precision in the lower ranges". Source: [XInput docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/xinput/getting-started-with-xinput).
- **Source, Xbox 360 config**: `joy_response_look 1` ("Acceleration Promotion") is a two-stage map. Off the rim, output = input × `joy_lowmap`/`joy_lowend`. Valve ships `joy_lowend 0.65` and `joy_lowmap 0.15`, so the inner area tops out at ≈17% of full rate (**derived**). Full speed comes only from the rim ramp in §1.3. Sources: [SDK2013 `in_joystick.cpp` L370–437](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/client/in_joystick.cpp#L370-L437), [360cfg](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-TF2/blob/91aeca3e0d124de6c24ed793e18d7cc487031df1/tf/cfg/360controller.cfg).
- **Call of Duty** offers Standard, Linear and Dynamic curves. Guides describe Standard as slow at first and faster toward the end of travel, Linear as 1:1, and Dynamic as fast at small deflection and smoother after. **Secondary**: guides only ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/call-of-duty/news/modern-warfare-aim-response-curve-types-explained)). I found no Activision definition.
- **Overwatch** offers Linear Ramp, Exponential Ramp and Dual-Zone (the default). Guides describe Dual-Zone as a slow inner zone covering about 90% of travel plus a fast outer zone. **Secondary**: community guides ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/overwatch-2-guide-aim-assist-options-possible-reasons-aiming-feels)). The idea is plausible, but the 90% figure is unconfirmed.

### 1.3 Turn acceleration: extra speed at the rim

Every system below works the same way. At or near full deflection, it adds turn speed after a delay and ramps that extra speed in over a set time. Leaving the rim resets the ramp.

- **Source (Valve, Xbox 360 config).** The stick counts as pegged when its length is at least `joy_pegged` **0.75**. Each axis pushed past `joy_accel_filter` **0.2** then ramps from `lowmap` 0.15 toward full at `joy_accelscale` **2.0 per second**. Nominal full rate therefore arrives ≈**0.5 s** after pegging (**derived**). Sources: [SDK2013 `in_joystick.cpp` L282, L369–437](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/client/in_joystick.cpp#L369-L437), [360cfg](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-TF2/blob/91aeca3e0d124de6c24ed793e18d7cc487031df1/tf/cfg/360controller.cfg). Caveat: the config ships `joy_accelmax 2.0`. With that value the SDK code keeps ramping past 1.0 until about 1 s, then drops back to 1.0. Read the code before copying it.
- **Overwatch (Blizzard, patch of 2025-12-09).** This patch added "Extra Horizontal Sensitivity at Max Deadzone", "Extra Vertical Sensitivity at Max Deadzone", "Extra Sensitivity Ramp-up delay (0 - 1 second)", "Extra Sensitivity Ramp-up time (0 - 1 second)" and "Disable Aim Assist at Max Deadzone". Source: [Overwatch patch notes, Dec 2025](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2025/12/).
- **Halo 5 (343).** "Look Acceleration" sets how quickly the camera reaches top speed with the stick fully pushed. The default is 3, and 2 matches the beta. 343 says the faster default "was done in part to help you make large aim corrections a bit faster". **Secondary**: press quoting 343 (links in §1.1).
- **Apex Legends ALC (Advanced Look Controls).** Hip and ADS each get their own Deadzone, Outer Threshold, Response Curve, Yaw Speed, Pitch Speed, Turning Extra Yaw, Turning Extra Pitch, Turning Ramp-up Time and Turning Ramp-up Delay. The names are confirmed by [GGRecon's Season 19 pro settings](https://www.ggrecon.com/guides/best-apex-legends-controller-settings-pros/), which lists hip yaw/pitch 300/260 and ADS 130/110 with all extras at 0. Guides read "extra" as added past the outer threshold, "ramp-up time" as the time to reach full extra, and "delay" as the wait before it starts. Respawn has not documented the units; they look like °/s. **Secondary.**
- **Fortnite** offers "Turning Horizontal/Vertical Boost", "Boost Ramp Time" and "Instant Boost When Building". **Secondary**: guides only.
- **Doom 3 BFG, legacy path.** `joy_dampenLook` capped how fast stick output could rise: `joy_deltaPerMSLook` 0.003 per ms, so 0 to full took ≈0.33 s (**derived**). The default path (`joy_newCode 1`) does not use it. Source: [D3BFG `UsercmdGen.cpp` L43–44, L552–650, L1017–1020](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/framework/UsercmdGen.cpp#L552-L650).

### 1.5 While aiming down sights

- **Source** multiplies stick (and mouse) turn rate by `fov / default_fov × zoom_sensitivity_ratio`, which is linear in field of view. Sources: [SDK2013 `view.cpp` L545–547](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/client/view.cpp#L545-L547), [`in_joystick.cpp` L814](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/client/in_joystick.cpp#L814).
- **Lyra** scales gamepad rate by `tan(fov/2) / tan(80°/2)`. Epic's comment calls this "the proper way to scale based off FOV changes". Lyra uses the same factor on aim-assist zones and caps, so assist "feels the same regardless of field of view". Source: [`AimAssistTargetManagerComponent.cpp` L106–119, L347–375](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistTargetManagerComponent.cpp#L347-L375).
- Lyra keeps separate gamepad presets for looking and for "targeting" (ADS): Slow 0.5, SlowPlus 0.75, SlowPlusPlus 0.9, Normal 1.0, NormalPlus 1.1, NormalPlusPlus 1.25, Fast 1.5, FastPlus 1.75, FastPlusPlus 2.0 and Insane 2.5. Both default to Normal. The mouse ADS multiplier defaults to 0.5. Sources: [`LyraAimSensitivityData.cpp` L14–23](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Source/LyraGame/Input/LyraAimSensitivityData.cpp#L14-L23), [`LyraSettingsShared.h` L330, L357–359](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Source/LyraGame/Settings/LyraSettingsShared.h#L330).

## 2. Aim assist

### 2.1 Vocabulary

- **Friction or slowdown**: the turn rate drops near a target. **Magnetism, rotational assist, adhesion or pull**: the camera follows a moving target. **Bullet magnetism or autoaim**: shots bend toward a target near the crosshair. **Centering or snap**: aim is moved onto the target, for example when you aim down sights.
- Insomniac's GDC 2013 talk on Resistance 3's aiming covers "camera acceleration and deadzoning" plus "magnetism, centering, and friction". I saw only the abstract; GDC Vault was in maintenance. Source: [Nick Weihs (Insomniac), "Techniques for Building Aim Assist in Console Shooters", GDC 2013](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017942/Techniques-for-Building-Aim-Assist).
- In Halo's terms, autoaim bends shots toward an enemy near the reticle, but only within the "red reticle range". Magnetism is controller-only: it slows turning inside a radius around the reticle and drifts the reticle with a moving target. **Secondary**: [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Aim_assist), a fan wiki citing 343's Halo 5 documentary series. It matches the patch-note terms in §2.3.
- A CHI 2014 study tested five techniques in an Unreal-based FPS. Most of them worked on a target range. Once game elements were added, only **bullet magnetism** and **area cursor** helped across situations, and some effective techniques were "too noticeable". Source: [Vicencio-Moreira, Mandryk, Gutwin, Bateman, "The Effectiveness (or Lack Thereof) of Aim-Assist Techniques in First-Person Shooter Games", CHI 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557308).

### 2.2 Open-source implementations with numbers

**Doom 3 BFG** ([`AimAssist.cpp`](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/AimAssist.cpp#L40-L62), [`Game_local.cpp` L1582–1595](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/Game_local.cpp#L1582-L1595))
- **Target choice.** Candidates are enemies within `aa_targetMaxDistance` 3000 u (≈76 m) that are in front of the player and visible at the torso (the head is a fallback). Each is scored 0.8 × view alignment + 0.2 × closeness ([L253–280](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/AimAssist.cpp#L253-L280)).
- **Friction.** `d` is how far the aim ray misses the target point, in world units. The friction radius is 96 u (≈2.4 m) up close. It widens linearly to 192 u at the "optimal" 768 u (≈19.5 m), then shrinks back to 96 u at 1024 u and beyond. Inside the radius, stick output is multiplied by lerp(**1.0 → 0.4**) as `d` goes to 0, so turning is up to 60% slower dead on target. The multiplier is applied *after* the response curve. For scale: the zone is ±14° at 19.5 m and ±1.8° at 76 m (**derived**). Sources: [L338–389](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/AimAssist.cpp#L338-L389), [`UsercmdGen.cpp` L758, L928](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/framework/UsercmdGen.cpp#L758).
- **Adhesion.** Adhesion only runs while the player moves faster than 10 u/s ([L120](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/AimAssist.cpp#L120)). Each tick it adds the yaw and pitch error, clamped to ±0.6°, times a contribution of clamp(1 − d/96, 0, **0.25**). That is at most 0.15° per tick, ≈9°/s at the engine's 60 Hz (**derived**). Sources: [L287–331](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/d3xp/AimAssist.cpp#L287-L331), [`Common.cpp` L83](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM-3-BFG/blob/1caba1979589971b5ed44e315d9ead30b278d8b4/neo/framework/Common.cpp#L83).
- The master switch `aa_targetAimAssistEnable` defaults to 0 in this PC release.

**Lyra (Epic)** ([`AimAssistInputModifier.cpp`](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistInputModifier.cpp#L109-L143), [`.h` L170–330](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Public/Input/AimAssistInputModifier.h#L170-L330), [`AimAssistTargetManagerComponent.cpp` L106–237](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistTargetManagerComponent.cpp#L106-L237))
- **Zones.** Zones are boxes in world space placed 30 m out (`ReticleDepth` 3000 cm). They are projected to the screen and FOV-scaled, so they keep a constant on-screen size. Inner is 20×20 cm (≈0.4° wide), outer 80×80 cm (≈1.5°) and targeting 1200×675 cm (≈23°×13°), all at the 80° base FOV (**derived**). A target counts when its projected bounds overlap a box. Up to 6 targets are considered within a 100 m range (FOV-scaled), each weighted by a time-on-target curve that lives in an asset.
- **Slow.** Turn rate is multiplied by (1 − strength). Strengths inner/outer are **0.6/0.5 hip** and **0.7/0.4 ADS**, which leaves 40%/50% of normal speed in hip and 30%/60% in ADS. "Dynamic slow" adds back the speed needed to follow the target whenever the stick pushes the same way the target moves, so slowdown never fights tracking. An optional floor, `SlowMinRotationRate`, is 0 (off) by default ([L704–750](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistInputModifier.cpp#L704-L750)).
- **Pull.** Pull adds strength × the rotation needed to follow the target given its own movement and the player's. It uses the same strengths as slow. It applies only while the look or move stick is outside its dead zone. With only the move stick in use, pull is scaled by the amount of strafe "to prevent view yanks when running forward past targets". An optional cap, `PullMaxRotationRate`, is 0 by default ([L665–702](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistInputModifier.cpp#L665-L702)).
- **Ramps.** Each strength moves toward its target at a constant **60/s** going in and **4/s** going out. In practice assist switches on in about 10 ms and fades over about 0.15 s (**derived**) ([L650–660](https://github.com/readyplayerme/Lyra-Sample/blob/b8474915c547d86c25476ff40ab025926e10cf10/Plugins/GameFeatures/ShooterCore/Source/ShooterCoreRuntime/Private/Input/AimAssistInputModifier.cpp#L650-L660)).

**Half-Life 2 / Source** ([`player.cpp` L7123–7215](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/server/player.cpp#L7123-L7215), [`baseentity.cpp` L3402–3408](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/server/baseentity.cpp#L3402-L3408), [`in_joystick.cpp` L235–256](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/client/in_joystick.cpp#L235-L256), [`hl2_player.cpp` L3035–3066](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/server/hl2/hl2_player.cpp#L3035-L3066), [360cfg](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-TF2/blob/91aeca3e0d124de6c24ed793e18d7cc487031df1/tf/cfg/360controller.cfg))
- **Bullet autoaim.** Each target is a disc facing the view. Its radius is **24 u (0.6 m) on PC** and **48 u (1.2 m) in console mode**, which is ≈3.5° and ≈7° half-angle at 10 m (**derived**). The best-scored target within 2160 u (55 m) bends the shot. The bend is clamped to ±25° pitch and ±12° yaw, then scaled by 0.9 ("always use non-sticky autoaim").
- **Console friction.** `joy_autoaimdampen` **0.5** halves stick input while the crosshair is on an autoaim target, but only while stick input is below `joy_autoaimdampenrange` **0.85**. A big flick is therefore never slowed. Autoaim state refreshes every 0.1 s.

### 2.3 Tuning in shipped games (patch notes)

- **Apex Legends, Season 22 (2024-08-05).** The notes read "Controller on PC: Aim Assist strength reduced 25%". Console players crossplaying into PC lobbies lost 18% (22% in performance mode), and console-only lobbies were unchanged. The dev note says "simply put, aim assist is too strong… Aim assist will never be removed as it's a critical accessibility feature." Source: [EA, Shockwave patch notes](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/apex-legends/news/shockwave-patch-notes).
  - Base strengths of **0.6 on console and 0.4 on PC** come from a YouTuber reading the game files in July 2020. **Secondary**: plausible, never confirmed ([Upcomer](https://upcomer.com/apex-legends-aim-assist-for-controller-is-supposedly-stronger-on-consoles-than-pc)). Respawn did confirm that the platforms have separate values when it posted that "aim assist on console was unintentionally set to PC values" ([@Respawn, 2021-11-03](https://twitter.com/Respawn/status/1456019263802601473)).
- **Call of Duty: Black Ops 7 (Treyarch, launch 2025-11-14).** Treyarch's data showed controller "slightly favored to win at close ranges" and keyboard-and-mouse favoured at long range. Black Ops 6 had already made rotational assist "scale over a short distance" at close range. BO7 increases "the range before full Rotational Aim Assist strength is achieved" and slightly raises strength "at very long ranges". It also requires the right stick to be "tracking an enemy target" for full strength, so using only the left stick gets reduced assist. The 2025-11-21 update cut the "Minimum Rotational Aim Assist penalty" from 35% to 25%. Source: [Black Ops 7 preseason patch notes](https://www.callofduty.com/patchnotes/2025/11/call-of-duty-black-ops-7-preseason-patch-notes).
- **Overwatch (Dec 2025).** The 2025-12-09 patch raised aim assist strength by 7% and added separate ease-in settings for projectile, hitscan and melee heroes. On 2025-12-18, "Aim Assist reduced to 4% down from 8%" for nine heroes on console. Source: [Overwatch patch notes, Dec 2025](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2025/12/).
- **Fortnite (v12.61 hotfix, May 2020).** Pull strengths for hip inner/outer went from 0.60→0.45 and 0.50→0.38; for ADS from 0.70→0.52 and 0.40→0.30. **Secondary**: dataminer Lucas7yoshi read these from hotfix config, and Epic did not announce them ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/fortnite-chapter-2-season-2-v12-61-patch-notes-aim-assist-nerfed-on-pc)). The values before the change exactly equal Lyra's defaults, which supports their authenticity.
- **Halo Infinite (Winter Update, Nov 2022).** The VK78 Commando's aim assist angle went from 5° to 6.25° and its bullet magnetism angle from 1.25° to 1.3°. The BR75's magnetism angle went from 2.4° to 2.2°, its magnetism falloff range from 12 to 10 wu, and its magnetism range from 20 to 18 wu. **Secondary**: [MP1st reproducing 343's notes](https://mp1st.com/news/halo-infinite-winter-update-balance-adjustments-include-stat-tuning-for-several-weapons); trust is high for the numbers. In March 2024, 343 reduced the Heatwave's aim assist angle, range and falloff range, and increased the Commando's "close range friction" ([343 blog repost](https://devtrackers.gg/halo/p/a4731857-march-update-preview-halo-infinite)).

### 2.4 Distance falloff

- **World-sized zones** shrink on screen with distance, so assist weakens far away on its own. Doom 3 uses a 96–192 u radius and HL2 a 24/48 u disc. Doom 3 deliberately widens the zone toward a mid-range "optimal" distance. See §2.2.
- **Screen-sized zones** stay the same size on screen at any distance; a range cutoff ends them. Lyra works this way (§2.2).
- **Close range** is where controller players win. Call of Duty therefore ramps rotational assist up over a short distance instead of applying full strength point-blank (§2.3).
- **Per-weapon** angle, range and falloff values are how Halo Infinite tunes assist (§2.3, secondary).

## 3. Game-feel techniques

| Technique | What it is / numbers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Latency budget | About 100 ms of response feels instant, and a perceive-and-correct cycle takes about 240 ms. Swink takes both figures from Card, Moran & Newell (1983). Swink defines game feel as real-time control + simulated space + polish | Steve Swink, *Game Feel* (Morgan Kaufmann, 2008). **Secondary for the numbers**: I saw them in [Bayreuth lecture slides citing the book, pp. 34 f.](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-07-Interfaces.pdf), not in the book itself |
| Screen shake by "trauma" | Keep a trauma value from 0 to 1. Events add to it (a hard hit +0.5, a small one +0.1 to 0.2), and it decays at a fixed linear rate. Shake = trauma² or trauma³, so stacked hits escalate. In 3D use **rotational shake only** (yaw/pitch/roll). Translational shake pushes the camera into walls and makes nearby geometry jitter. Drive each axis with Perlin noise on its own seed, which is frame-rate independent and works in slow motion. Shake a copy of the camera, never the base camera. His 2D demo used a 10° maximum | [Squirrel Eiserloh, "Math for Game Programmers: Juicing Your Cameras With Math", GDC 2016](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tu-Qe66AvtY) |
| Camera kick on fire | Push the camera opposite to the shot direction, on top of random shake. Also add "gun kick" (the weapon visibly recoils) and "gun delay" (the gun lags the view slightly) | [Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer), "The Art of Screenshake", INDIGO Classes 2013](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U) |
| Shake needs an off switch | Nuclear Throne added an option to turn screen shake off "because some people were getting really nauseous" | Nijman 2013, Q&A |
| View punch that springs back (recoil recovery) | Source decays view punch as a damped spring: damping 9 /s and spring 65 /s². That gives ≈12% overshoot and settles in ≈0.9 s (**derived**: ω ≈ 8.1 rad/s, ζ ≈ 0.56). HL2's SMG fires 13.3 shots/s and kicks each shot by 0.2° plus up to 1.0° more as the burst nears 2 s. Sideways kick is a third of that with a random sign, and there is a small roll. Each kick is scaled ×0.5, the total punch is clipped to 24°/3°/1°, and easy mode halves everything | [SDK2013 `gamemovement.cpp` L1203–1240](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/shared/gamemovement.cpp#L1203-L1240), [`basehlcombatweapon.cpp` L158–201](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/server/hl2/basehlcombatweapon.cpp#L158-L201), [`weapon_smg1.cpp` L46, L306–318](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/be57e3d01ac7d7aa6a2cacbfef187c0b46616db0/src/game/server/hl2/weapon_smg1.cpp#L306-L318) |
| No flinch when hit | Apex removed "Aim Flinch" from all weapons and most abilities ("no one really had love for Aim Flinch"). It kept flinch for ring damage and a few abilities, where the feedback helps | [Apex Season 22 notes](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/apex-legends/news/shockwave-patch-notes) |
| Visual hit feedback | An impact effect on every hit, walls included. Enemies flash white and play a hit animation, with a little knockback. Bigger and faster bullets, and a muzzle flash (the first bullet frame drawn as a circle). Lower enemy HP and a higher rate of fire. Permanence: shells, corpses and debris stay | Nijman 2013 |
| | Flash white on hit, add particles, tween and squash things | [Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho, "Juice it or lose it", 2012](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg) |
| Hit-stop ("sleep") | Pause the game for about **20 ms** on a hit and on a kill. Nijman: "your brain won't notice that but kind of use that time to process" | Nijman 2013 |
| Hit sound | TF2's optional hit "ding" plays at a different pitch for minor and major damage hits (convars added 2010-01-13). Jonasson & Purho call sound "the most cost-effective thing you can add". Nijman tells a secondhand story of a Raven gun fixed with a +12 dB bass boost | [TF2 update 2010-01-13](https://store.steampowered.com/news/3330); Juice 2012; Nijman 2013 |
| Hit markers can leak information | Ubisoft removed hit markers from Rainbow Six Siege, except for kills and friendly fire, because players "were exploiting the feature to fish for hits through walls and smoke". Bigger blood splatter replaced them | [Ubisoft, "Closed Alpha Feedback"](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/rainbow-six/siege/news-updates/4ZXqtA5sxazw8Npy34Tsxw/closed-alpha-feedback) |
| Smoothing | `x += (target − x) * 0.1` each frame (asymptotic averaging). This is frame-rate dependent, so convert it to `1 − exp(−k·dt)` at reVC's frame rate | Juice 2012; Eiserloh 2016 |
| Coyote time / input buffer | Celeste still accepts a jump 0.1 s after the player leaves a ledge (`JumpGraceTime = 0.1f`) and buffers jump presses made just before landing. The buffer length is not in the public source. Applying the same idea to fire, reload or ADS presses made late in a weapon swap is **judgment** | [Celeste `Player.cs` L48](https://github.com/NoelFB/Celeste/blob/1b0ce45c75e05649ae91b44a8bb6b196684e4352/Source/Player/Player.cs#L48); buffering per Thorson's 2020 thread, **secondary** ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/celeste-dev-explains-how-they-made-their-game-feel/1100-6474775/)) |
